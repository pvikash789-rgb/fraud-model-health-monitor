Fraud Model Health Monitor

v0.1 prototype

Fraud changes faster than fraud models. This tool scores every transaction with a predictive fraud model, then shows how quickly that model goes stale when a new attack appears, how long monitoring takes to notice, and how retraining and network-shared fraud signals win the losses back.

Runs entirely in your browser on synthetic card transactions, so it is free and needs no bank data to try. Pick an institution type, launch an attack, and compare five retraining policies on the same traffic. The same monitor works on any issuer, neobank or card network that can export scores and fraud labels.

Network intelligence

Learning from fraud other banks have already seen

A single bank only learns about a new attack from its own losses, after the label delay. A card network or fraud consortium sees the same attack hit other banks first. In the network-informed policy, confirmed fraud from peer banks (as anonymised signals, never customer identities) joins this bank’s training data. When enough arrives, a challenger is trained and promoted if it is no worse on the bank’s own traffic.

Model registry: champion vs challenger

Drift-triggered policy

An alarm does not ship a model on its own. Each alarm trains a challenger out of time: it learns from labelled data that stops 7 days short, then both models are scored on those held-back 7 days. The challenger is promoted only if it beats the live champion on PR-AUC by at least 0.01. Otherwise the champion stays and nothing is deployed.

How it works and how a bank would use it

    

      
The data
Each day has 3,000 sampled card transactions with eight signals: amount, time of day, card present or not, device, velocity, merchant type, location match and account age. A baseline fraud pattern runs throughout. On the attack day a new pattern ramps in over a week. Fraud rates are boosted in the sample so the patterns are visible, then rescaled to the institution’s real volume and fraud rate for the money figures.

      
The model
A logistic regression with pairwise feature interactions, trained on the latest 45 days of labelled data. Its decision threshold is set so it flags exactly the alert budget. Retraining only ever sees labels that have actually arrived, which is why label delay matters so much.

      
The monitor
Two alarms. A performance alarm compares the catch rate on matured labels with what the model expected at training time. A traffic alarm tracks the population stability index (PSI) of today’s scores against the training reference and fires after two days above 0.2. Alarms are spaced at least 7 days apart, and each one must pass a champion/challenger gate before anything ships.

Product spec
If this were a real platform feature

    

      
Problem
Fraud models decay silently. Teams find out weeks later from losses, then retrain on a fixed calendar that is either too slow for a new attack or wasteful the rest of the time. Nobody can say in money terms what a retrain was worth.

      
Users
  • Fraud data scientists who own model performance
  • ML platform engineers who run retraining and deployment
  • Heads of fraud risk who answer for losses and declines

      
North Star metric
Fraud value caught at a fixed decline budget. It rewards better models without letting anyone buy recall by declining more customers.

      
Guardrails
  • False declines per 10,000 good transactions
  • Alert rate stays within budget after every promotion
  • Retrains per quarter and compute spend
  • Share of challengers rejected (too many means noisy alarms)

      
Roadmap
  • Now: simulator, monitor, champion/challenger gate, incident payload
  • Next: upload a bank’s own scored transactions in the schema above and replay its history
  • Later: shadow-mode challengers on live traffic, and an LLM agent that drafts the retraining ticket and validation report
